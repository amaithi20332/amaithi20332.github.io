    Research

    Research that shaped the roadmap

    Two pieces of research I put together for Know Your Spends and iFinance: a feature benchmark of banks and apps, and a primer on the personal finance industry.

    Personal finance benchmarking study

    2024 · 6 Indian banks (including ICICI) and 13 global personal finance apps, compared feature by feature

    I compared spend tracking, budgeting, bill and subscription tracking, and account linking across Indian banks and global apps such as Mint, Quicken, YNAB, Emma, Empower and Jupiter.

    Indian banks that let users link accounts held elsewhere

    Each bar is one of the 6 banks studied, at the time of the study.

    Key findings
    • No Indian bank showed investments or loans held at other institutions; global leaders did
    • Only half the banks offered real-time spend categorization, and only a third offered smart budgeting
    • Global apps stood out with subscription tracking, net-worth calculators and receipt scanning
    What it led to: stocks and ETFs from every broker in iFinance, and the Financial Dashboard, which brings investments, loans and insurance into one view.

    A primer on how personal finance tools evolved, from household ledgers to Quicken and Microsoft Money, then Mint and mobile apps, and now AI and open banking.

    Experiments

    Switch it off to prove it works

    I pause features on purpose to measure their real effect. These results decided what we kept.

    Push notifications: growth in users in one month +219%
    When we paused them, users fell 29% the next month, so notifications stayed on.
    In-app tooltip: growth in accounts linked over two weeks +19%
    Removing it cut accounts linked by 39%.
    Investment data-fetch success after chunking ~0% → 88%
    Splitting long history requests into smaller time windows fixed the main depositories.
